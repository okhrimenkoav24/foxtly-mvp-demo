The rule set behind the demo

What makes a campaign managed

  • Created inside Foxtly — managed from birth.
  • Customer clicks Take under management on an existing campaign.
  • Customer accepts a Foxy optimization proposal for it.
  • Customer generates AI creatives for it.
  • Stored as campaigns.management_mode = managed with managed_source and managed_at.

What counts against the cap

  • Only active managed campaigns. Paused and archived are excluded.
  • Everything else in the ad account is read-only analytics: free, unlimited, included on every plan.
  • The customer controls the count: pause or release, and a slot frees immediately.
  • Caps: START 3 · GROWTH 10 · PRO unlimited (active_campaigns_cap = null).

Where the block lives

  • Server-side, at two transitions: analytics → managed-active, and resume of a paused managed campaign.
  • The UI hides nothing — the button stays visible and opens the upgrade dialog.
  • Campaign launch inside Foxtly is gated at the same check, before the platform call.
  • Reads and reports are never gated.

Downgrade and over-limit

  • Nothing is auto-paused on downgrade. Ads keep running.
  • 7-day grace period with a persistent banner to pick which campaigns stay managed.
  • After the grace period Foxtly releases the excess, lowest 30-day spend first, and notifies by email.
  • Released means Foxtly stops optimizing — the campaign keeps running in the ad account.

Open decisions this demo assumes

  • Grace period on downgrade: 7 days, then automatic release by lowest spend. Alternative considered: block the downgrade until the customer reduces usage. Rejected — it traps the customer in a higher price.
  • AI creative overage sells a pack (+$39 / 30 creatives) before it offers an upgrade, because the quota resets monthly and a plan change is the heavier commitment.
  • The campaign cap is not enforced on PRO — null, not a large number, so a future PRO ceiling is a data change rather than a code change.
  • Trial (GROWTH, 10 days) uses GROWTH caps unchanged. No separate trial limits.
